Start with the number itself. Search for your own number in quotation marks, including the version with the country code and the local format you’ve used. Searching your name can miss a reverse phone lookup page, an old ad, or a listing under the name of a business you used to run.

Save the URLs where the number appears. A single number can show up in the text, inside a PDF, or on a button labelled **Call**, so open the result and check where the number actually lives.

## Remove the number from pages you control

Start with public profiles, listings, posts and documents you can edit. Change the public contact field or remove the post if you no longer need it. Open the updated page while signed out and check the contact button. A site can display updated text while the button still points at your old number. On desktop, you can use **Copy link address** on the button and check the link without placing a call.

For example, an old event page might show a general contact inbox today and still have a downloadable flyer with your mobile number. Update the flyer and check its old direct URL as well as the event page. Review any number you use for account recovery separately so you can preserve access to the account.

## Opt out of reverse phone lookup and people-search sites

If your number appears on a people-search site, follow that site’s opt-out process for the listing. Use the exact profile URL and note the date. Look for other entries with the same number, including old profiles with a previous city or spelling of your name. When a separate directory or community site publishes the number, use its correction form or ask the publisher to take it off that page. [FTC guide to people-search opt-outs](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-people-search-sites-sell-your-information) · [Copy a data broker opt-out request]({% post_url 2026-11-17-data-broker-opt-out-request-template %})

If the number is new to you and a page lists it under someone else’s name, explain that the number now reaches you. Give the site the URL and ask it to correct that phone field. Use the site’s official contact route if its form requires you to verify the name on the profile.

If the caller keeps asking about a job you left, check the business directories that still have that role on file. [How to opt out of Apollo and ZoomInfo]({% post_url 2026-11-19-apollo-zoominfo-opt-out %})

## Remove your number from Google Search

While a live result shows your personal phone number, you can ask Google to remove the eligible result. Open its three-dot menu and choose **Remove result** when available, or follow Google’s contact-info removal instructions. Google handles visibility in Search, and the publisher controls the page. [Google’s personal contact information removal guide](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/12719076?hl=en)

If the source has removed the number and Google still shows the old text, submit that URL for a refresh. Check the page again after a response, including any call link or separate download that originally exposed the number.

# Your Grandma Got Scammed Because a Company Sold Her First

I've spent over ten years in cybersecurity, offensive research, defensive operations, security engineering. I've broken into systems, watched attackers work, and rebuilt the walls after. And I am tired of hearing that grandma got scammed because she was "too trusting" or "not tech savvy."

No. She got scammed because a company sold her.

## The Numbers Nobody Wants to Say Out Loud

US seniors lost **$7.7 billion** to fraud in a single year, 37% of all US cybercrime losses, while making up a far smaller share of the population. (Source: Elder Fraud Statistics, Axis Intelligence, Oct 2026)

There are **603 registered data brokers** in California alone, and Californians have already filed **58 million deletion requests** trying to claw their lives back. (Source: Data Broker Statistics 2026, Axis Intelligence)

In the UK, people aged 60+ filed **26% of all identity theft reports** in 2022. The harm isn't random. It concentrates on older people. (Source: Elder Fraud Statistics, WifiTalents, Feb 2026)

The FBI now files grandparent scams under confidence/romance fraud, and "distress" scams, voice-clone calls included, racked up over $5 million in losses in 2025 alone. The scammers aren't guessing anymore. They're working from a dossier.

## How It Actually Works

Nobody cold-calls 10,000 grandmas and hopes. That's not how this works anymore. Here's the pipeline:

1. A data broker buys or scrapes public records, marketing lists, loyalty programs, app data. Your grandma's name, age, address, phone number, relatives, property value. All of it, linked, profiled, and priced.
2. A scammer buys that list, filtered by age, by neighborhood, by net worth. Grandma is on a spreadsheet next to a column that says what her house is worth.
3. A voice clone of her grandson takes five minutes and a few public clips. The broker's profile supplies the details that make the call land: the grandson's name, the town, the school.
4. The call comes at 2 a.m. "Grandma, I'm in jail, please don't tell Mom." Every detail is right because a company sold every detail.

The scammer is the trigger. The data broker loaded the gun.

## "Just Opt Out" Is a Scam of Its Own

The industry's own numbers prove it: **1.5% of deletion requests are denied outright**, and two-thirds of those denials come from just five companies. (Source: Data Broker Statistics 2026, Axis Intelligence) That's the polite refusals. The sneaky ones come back as "we couldn't match your request to a record," which means they keep the file and you can't prove otherwise.

I've written before about how to [opt out and follow up](/posts/2026/11/17/data-broker-opt-out-request-template/), how to [use California's DROP portal](/posts/2026/11/17/california-drop-data-broker-deletion/), and what to do when a broker [demands your driver's licence to delete a listing](/posts/2026/11/19/data-broker-opt-out-id-verification/). Do all of it. But know the deck is stacked: opt-outs are whack-a-mole, records reappear, and the burden of proof lands on the victim, not the seller.

## What Actually Protects Her

- **Freeze her credit** at all three bureaus. It's free, it's reversible, and it stops account-opening fraud cold.
- **Set the callback rule.** Any call about money or family emergencies gets a callback on a known number. No exceptions, no matter how real the voice sounds.
- **Opt her out** of the big people-search sites, then check again in three months. Set a calendar reminder. This is maintenance, not a one-time fix.
- **Talk about the voice clone out loud.** Tell her the technology exists, tell her it's cheap, and tell her a real grandson will never be angry about a callback.

## Why I Take This Personally

I've spent a decade in this field, and I've watched the industry build a supply chain for cruelty. Every "people search" site, every "marketing insights" firm, every app that sells location data is a link in the chain that ends with a scared 80-year-old wiring her savings to a stranger who knows her grandson's name.

The scammers are criminals. But the industry that armed them is legal, registered, and profitable. That's the part that should keep you up at night.
